Why Bakersfield Roofing Pros

We Roof for the Heat First

A Bakersfield roof spends four months a year under 95-degree sun, and the shingle that goes back on decides how much of that heat lands in the attic. We start with what the sun is doing to your attic and your cooling bill, then talk shingle color, cool-roof granules, ventilation, and whether your tile is sound enough to relay.

Cooler Attic, Smaller Bill

Cool-roof shingles and coatings reflect more sun than a dark roof and run cooler through July. We explain what that means for your attic and your air conditioner, in plain numbers where the sources have them.

Tile Done Properly

On many Bakersfield tile roofs the tile is fine and the paper under it is gone. We lift the field tile, lay new underlayment and flashings, and set the sound tile back.

Permit and Title 24 Handled

Roof replacement is permitted work in the City of Bakersfield and in Kern County. We file it, and the cool-roof paperwork the energy code asks for goes in with it.

How a Job Runs

From Roof Walk to Final Walk in 4 Steps

The same order on every Bakersfield job, whether it is a pipe boot or a full tile relay.

Roof Walk With Photos

We walk every slope, open the attic hatch and photograph what we find: granule loss, cracked tile, slipped underlayment, cooked flashing, blocked vents.

Written Scope and Materials

You get the photos, a written scope and a price, with the material choices laid out: standard shingle, cool-roof shingle, coating, or a tile relay.

Permit, Then Roof Day

We file the City or Kern County permit, order material, and schedule the tear-off or lift-and-relay for a day you agree to.

Final Walk and Attic Check

When the roof is on we walk it with you, check the attic for daylight and vent flow, and hand over the permit sign-off paperwork.

Straight Answers

Bakersfield Roofing Questions, Answered Plainly

Heat, tile, code and cost, answered for Kern County homes.

Local cost guides put a full replacement on most Bakersfield single-family homes somewhere between $10,000 and $25,000, and many Central Valley projects land around $15,000 to $20,000. Where your house falls depends on the material (architectural shingle, cool-roof shingle or tile), the square count, how many layers come off and what the decking looks like once it is bare. The roof walk is how we turn that market range into a number for your address.
Three is plenty if each one is written after someone has actually been on the roof. Look for the shingle or tile product named, the underlayment named, the flashing and vent work spelled out, the permit listed as part of the job, and a line on what happens if bad decking turns up. A quote that skips the permit or the underlayment is a quote that will grow.
Usually, yes, because the tile you already own goes back on. The cost in a relay is labor, new underlayment, flashings, battens where needed and replacing the tile that cracks in handling. A new tile roof adds the price of all new tile on top of that. We quote a relay per job after the walk, since the amount of broken tile and the condition of the deck change the labor.
The gap usually comes from what each quote leaves out. One quote assumes a single tear-off layer, another finds two. One prices a standard shingle, another a cool-roof shingle that satisfies Title 24. One includes new pipe boots and flashings, another plans to reuse the old ones. Line the quotes up by scope first and the price differences start to explain themselves.
Tile handles the heat better over the long run, shingle handles the budget better up front. Concrete tile sits on battens with an air gap beneath it, which slows heat moving into the attic, and the tile itself commonly lasts 40 years and more here. An architectural shingle roof in Bakersfield commonly gives 15 to 25 years, and a cool-roof shingle closes some of the heat gap at a lower cost than tile. Your roof framing also votes: tile is heavy, and not every truss was built for it.
